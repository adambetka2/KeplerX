# Scientific references

Papers on KBonus photometry, transit detection, vetting and calibration. The
survey protocol is [survey-protocol.md](survey-protocol.md); the detector
programme is [dwarf-programme.md](dwarf-programme.md).

- Martínez-Palomera et al. (2023), [Kepler Bonus: Light Curves of Kepler Background Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17733) — the core dataset paper, including the 406,548 newly extracted background-source light curves.
- Martínez-Palomera et al. (2021 preprint), [Kepler Bonus: Aperture Photometry Light Curves of EXBA Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06683) — earlier KBonus background photometry and a planet-candidate discovery.
- Hedges et al. (2021), [Linearized Field Deblending: PSF Photometry for Impatient Astronomers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08411) — the deblending and photometric extraction method underlying KBonus.
- Bienias & Szabó (2025), [Background exoplanet candidates in the original Kepler field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09152) — directly relevant prior searches and candidate identification in background pixels.
- Twicken et al. (2016), [Detection of Potential Transit Signals in 17 Quarters of Kepler Data: Results of the Final Kepler Mission Transiting Planet Search (DR25)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06140) — the final mission transit search and detection-consistency tests.
- Thompson et al. (2018), [Planetary Candidates Observed by Kepler. VIII. A Fully Automated Catalog With Measured Completeness and Reliability Based on Data Release 25](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06758) — Robovetter, candidate dispositions, completeness, and reliability.
- Christiansen et al. (2020), [Measuring Transit Signal Recovery in the Kepler Pipeline IV: Completeness of the DR25 Planet Candidate catalog](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04796) — pixel-level injections and empirical detection efficiency.
- Kovács, Zucker & Mazeh (2002), [A box-fitting algorithm in the search for periodic transits](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0206099) — the foundational Box Least Squares (BLS) transit-search algorithm.
- Hippke & Heller (2019), [Transit Least Squares: Optimized transit detection algorithm to search for periodic transits of small planets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02015) — physically shaped transit searches and injection-recovery comparisons.
- Hippke et al. (2019), [Wotan: Comprehensive time-series de-trending in Python](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00966) — detrending benchmarks focused on preserving shallow planetary transits.
- Shallue & Vanderburg (2018), [Identifying Exoplanets with Deep Learning: A Five Planet Resonant Chain around Kepler-80 and an Eighth Planet around Kepler-90](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05044) — neural transit vetting and searches for overlooked Kepler planets.
- Valizadegan et al. (2022), [ExoMiner: A Highly Accurate and Explainable Deep Learning Classifier that Validates 301 New Exoplanets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10009) — classification using transit and diagnostic information.
- Morton et al. (2016), [False Positive Probabilities for All Kepler Objects of Interest: 1284 Newly Validated Planets and 428 Likely False Positives](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02825) — astrophysical false-positive calculations and statistical validation.
- Vanderburg & Johnson (2014), [A Technique for Extracting Highly Precise Photometry for the Two-Wheeled Kepler Mission](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.3853) — K2SFF pointing-systematics correction.
- Luger et al. (2017 preprint), [An update to the EVEREST K2 pipeline: Short cadence, saturated stars, and Kepler-like photometry down to Kp = 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05488) — EVEREST 2.0 pixel-level decorrelation for K2 photometry.
- Kruse et al. (2019), [Detection of Hundreds of New Planet Candidates and Eclipsing Binaries in K2 Campaigns 0–8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10806) — a systematic K2 transit search and candidate catalog.
- Zink et al. (2020), [Scaling K2. II. Assembly of a Fully Automated C5 Planet Candidate Catalog Using EDI-Vetter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11515) — automatic vetting calibrated with transit injections before detrending and inverted-light-curve noise controls.
- Zink et al. (2021), [Scaling K2. IV. A Uniform Planet Sample for Campaigns 1–8 and 10–18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02675) — a homogeneous K2 search with completeness and reliability characterization.
- Heller, Hippke & Rodenbeck (2019), [Transit least-squares survey II: Discovery and validation of 17 new sub- to super-Earth-sized planets in multi-planet systems from K2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09038) — recovering small planets missed by earlier searches.
- Paz et al. (2026), [VarWISE: Infrared Variability via NEOWISE Single Exposure Photometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19059) — the requested NEOWISE paper using VARnet for variability detection; a methodological reference beyond transit searches.
